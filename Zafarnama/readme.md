## Zafarnama - Guru Gobind Singh

In my third essay, [The Collapse of the Borrowed Self](https://open.substack.com/pub/kkumar420/p/the-collapse-of-the-borrowed-self), I talk at length about the immense difficulties Guru Gobind Singh Sahib endured — the Siege of Anandpur, the Battle of Chamkaur, the loss of all four sons (the Chaar Sahibzaade) along with his kin and followers to the forces of Aurangzeb, following which he was left alone to wander in the jungle of Macchiwara. It was in the aftermath of this devastation that he composed the Zafarnama — an epistle of spiritual and moral victory addressed to Aurangzeb himself, in which he rebukes him for the betrayal and slaughter with unflinching honesty, exposes his broken oaths and hollow piety, and does so without a trace of despair, hatred, or self-pity. He asserts instead his complete and unshaken trust in the One Lord who has guided and protected him regardless of outcome. The letter stands as a rare and striking testimony to how one who has realized the non-dual Absolute meets catastrophe: not with grief or vengeance, but with a serenity that seems to regard the entire episode from somewhere beyond it.

Throughout the letter, Guru Gobind Sahib speaks of the Divine who is formless and beyond the reach of duality. He sets aside everything Aurangzeb prizes — armies, wealth, thrones — as trivial beside the protection of that one Lord, secure not through any personal strength but through total surrender of the personal self. He calls the world an inn, whose dwellers merely pass through it, and meets Aurangzeb's cruelty and broken faith not with anger but as one already reconciled to loss, having relinquished any private stake in the outcome. It is this complete submission to the Absolute, the Ik Onkar — untouched by fear, grief, or the wish for revenge — that makes the Zafarnama one of the most striking literary expressions of non-dual realization under the worst possible circumstances.
